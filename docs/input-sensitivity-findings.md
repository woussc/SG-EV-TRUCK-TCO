# EV Truck TCO - input sensitivity and usability findings

_Generated by `node tools/sensitivity.mjs` (deterministic, offline). Engine is frozen; this script only clones inputs in memory and never edits fixtures or the engine._

## What was tested

A one-at-a-time (tornado) sweep against the fully-specified illustrative scenario **Wins case (fully specified, gates pass)**, whose best EV plan clears every gate so its Total economic TCO and Saving % are numeric and responsive. Each driver was perturbed +/-10% (or an additive +/-0.03/yr for rates that sit at 0 in the data), and the resulting swing in the two business-critical outputs was measured:

1. **Total economic TCO** of the best EV plan (the account-size / headline number).
2. **Saving % vs Keep ICE** of the best EV plan (the adoption-case number).

**Baseline:** best EV plan **PLAN_A**, Total economic TCO **S$7,867,090**, Saving % **29.43%**, Keep ICE total **S$11,148,680**.

## Usability sanity checks

- PASS - Best EV plan Total economic TCO is numeric
- PASS - Saving % vs Keep ICE is numeric
- PASS - Keep ICE total is numeric and positive
- PASS - Higher EV quote raises Total economic TCO (monotone)
- PASS - Lower EV quote reduces Total economic TCO (monotone)
- PASS - Halving diesel price reduces EV Saving %
- PASS - Plan A EV-cost slider recomputes Plan A vehicle TCO
- PASS - Plan B EV-cost slider recomputes Plan B vehicle TCO
- PASS - Plan A EV-cost slider changes ONLY Plan A (Plan B TCO unchanged)
- PASS - Plan B EV-cost slider changes ONLY Plan B (Plan A TCO unchanged)
- PASS - Plan A (SRM) and Plan B (competitor) differ at equal EV price

**Overall: model is usable - all sanity checks pass.**

## Notes on the measured zeros (not "no effect in principle")

A few drivers show a near-zero swing **in this scenario** for structural reasons, not because they are unimportant in general:

- **Vehicle grant (per EV)** shows 0 because the per-truck grant only reduces TCO when its *grant timing month* falls inside the horizon, and the illustrative case leaves that month blank. It is a conditional, manually-checked incentive: when its timing is supplied it directly lowers Day-0 funding and Total TCO. Keep it visible but grouped with the incentive inputs.
- **Discount rate** and **diesel-price growth** read ~0 because the demonstration macro settings are inflation = 0 and discount = 0 (NPV = nominal). They become material the moment a non-zero escalation/discount is entered. They are finance assumptions, so they belong behind an Advanced disclosure with their zero defaults preserved.
- **EV maintenance override** reads 0 here because the illustrative EVs price maintenance from the supplied 200,000 km service schedule rather than the flat override. It still matters when the override is the active basis.
- **COE months left** and **Installed chargers** show n.a./0 because a relative +/-10% nudge lands them on a non-integer or crosses a discrete gate (replacement-timing band, integer charger count). They are still meaningful levers; they are just not smooth under a continuous sweep. Replacement timing (COE months left) remains a primary duty input.

## Interpretation for UI curation

The high-impact drivers (top of the ranking) are the ones that should be promoted to an always-visible **primary input** set in the toggleable sidebar. The low-impact drivers (bottom of the ranking, near-zero swing, plus the conditional/finance items above) are rarely-changed fine print that can be demoted behind an **Advanced** disclosure, keeping their current demonstration defaults so every computed number is unchanged and `python tools/validate.py` still passes 19/19.

> Note on what shipped: the **Advanced** disclosure demotes only the low-impact *operating-cost rows* and the detailed *per-charger energy/site line items*. The fleet-register and replacement-plan tables stay fully visible in the sidebar, so fleet/replacement columns (including each truck's year-10 / terminal values and replacement months) are kept primary by virtue of the whole table being shown, not demoted into Advanced.

### Proposed primary (always-visible) inputs

1. EV purchase quote per truck (Replacement plan, col D) - the single biggest lever (~14% impact).
2. New ICE purchase quote (Replacement plan, col C) - sets the Keep/New baseline (~6%).
3. Commercial diesel price and ICE fuel consumption L/km (Energy) - together the diesel running-cost lever (~5-6% each).
4. Work km/day and Days/year worked (Fleet register) - duty intensity (~3% each).
5. Depot electricity price and EV energy use kWh/km (Energy) - EV running-cost lever (~2% each).
6. EV and ICE insurance (OpCost) - the largest recurring fixed costs (~2%).
7. COE months left (Fleet register) - replacement timing, a primary duty decision even though it is discrete.
8. Charger equipment price and installed chargers (Site) - site build-up scale.
9. Vehicle grant and its timing (Incentive) - conditional but directly lowers TCO when set.

### Proposed Advanced (collapsed) inputs

Everything with <~1% impact and rarely changed in a first-pass conversation: tyres, road tax, inspection, contract surcharges, unavailable-days/backup rates, battery reserve/fade, auxiliary energy, price-growth and discount rates, terminal values, repair-timing months, data-mode/quote-basis flags, and the detailed per-charger site line items beyond equipment price. Each keeps its current demonstration default, so hiding it changes no computed number.
