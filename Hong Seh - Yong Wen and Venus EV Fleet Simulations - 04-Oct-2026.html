Do not present these fleet counts as owned-vehicle facts. Yong Wen reports 20 delivery vehicles, but their Singapore allocation, ownership and temperature split are not established. Research priority: Yong Wen has the stronger public signal of an operating delivery fleet. It is the better first interview candidate for the ambient lane. Venus offers clearer delivery geography, but ownership and central replenishment scope must be confirmed before treating it as a truck buyer.

The numerical base assumes new COEs, L2EV trucks registered in October 2026, S$180,000 gross all-in EV cost and S$160,000 matched new ICE cost. Neither price is a verified Hong Seh or customer quotation. These prices can change the decision.

6.2 Space and the S$500,000 claim

11. Calculation approach, validation and limits

11.1 Operation-to-cost formulas

Productive demand = routes/day × route km × dispatch days; stops and cargo checked separately
Diesel visits = ceiling(work km × available days × L/km / (refill litres − detour km × L/km))
Diesel quantity = (productive km on available days + refuel visits × detour km) × L/km
EV grid intensity = battery kWh/km / (1 − charging loss)
Public visits = ceiling(public share × route/auxiliary grid demand /
                        (grid refill energy − public share × charger detour energy))
EV grid quantity = productive route/auxiliary grid demand + public visits × detour energy
Extra paid time = (public/attended charging + station detour/queue/connect
                   + depot plug handling + other extra time) × paid-time share
Unattended overnight depot charging hours are not charged as driver labour
Vehicle TCO = current asset value + purchases + old/new operations + repairs + sale fees
              − old vehicle disposal − year-10 assets − conditional in-horizon vehicle grants
Site TCO = initial installed cost + recurring cost + renewal + restoration − recovery
Fleet TCO = sum of truck-path TCO + shared site TCO, counted once
Saving % = (named ICE baseline TCO − EV-plan TCO) / named ICE baseline TCO
15% headroom = 0.85 × named baseline TCO − EV-plan TCO

Validation result: 24 scenarios × four paths = 96 plan results. 2,690 native-versus-independent numeric and ledger checks passed; 0 failed. This covers totals, component sums, annual cash, current-asset bridges, funding, grants and both ICE baselines. The original v4 workbook hash is unchanged.

Two agents researched the companies separately. A third agent challenged the model, profile boundaries and ledger logic. The main agent built one shared scenario set, ran disposable native Excel copies, reconciled independent arithmetic and compiled this report. Agents used the inherited session model, with bounded subtasks; no separate calculator was built for each company.

Confidence distinction: numerical reconciliation is strong. Company-fit confidence is low because ownership, manifest, prices, COEs and charger rights are missing. A pass count is not a probability that a company will save money. No statistical confidence interval or invented confidence score is assigned.

11.2 Known model limitations and safeguards applied

  • v4 has one per-truck depot mode and one homogeneous charger bank per plan. AC and DC are tested in separate consistent scenarios; multi-depot sharing is not assumed.
  • Vehicle grants are single-event inputs in v4. Native runs set them to zero; the external ledger uses the three verified policy events and labels adjusted results separately.
  • v4 does not fully check public access for mixed charging, or public-only start timing. This report independently treats all public-share access as open and requires availability before first EV duty.
  • v4’s ICE-life service check can affect an EV path, and its consumption guard is incomplete. All entered lives are 120 months; consumption is positive and manually checked.
  • Annual downtime is averaged across full and Saturday dispatch days. Backup cost assumes the same service is delivered and includes substitute fuel. Actual peak disruption and route coverage need dispatch evidence.
  • Maintenance budgets are flat annual assumptions, not kilometre-specific service schedules. Repair reserve is not a full battery price or a promise of availability.
  • EV road tax uses the full 2028 rate throughout. It overstates the early-year rate for this 2026 start by a small amount; it does not model a future tax waiver.
  • Charging loss is applied once to battery-side consumption. Future efficiency change, taper, weather, batch scheduling and charge-loss growth are not predicted.
  • The aged battery route gate assumes no mid-route charge and no capacity reset from the repair reserve. A new battery or second charging session requires a new operational test.
  • Normal wages, revenue, income tax, financing cost and recoverable GST are not part of the displayed TCO. Material changes in overtime, contracts or customer service must be added; savings cannot be created by shifting cost to a driver.

v4’s native cash-style ledger includes annual space opportunity value. This report separately removes that non-cash allowance from its cash-outlay estimate, while retaining it in economic TCO. Both values are labelled. The site field “Next site confirmed” is No because no continuation site is established. Entered tenure covers the modeled horizon, so no continuation is needed within this hypothetical case. The field does not confirm a charger-renewal quotation.

Meter boundary must be consistent: this simulation uses grid-side kWh and charging power after applying charging loss once. Public invoices may measure charger-output kWh instead. Confirm the invoice meter and measured power boundary before inserting real tariffs; otherwise the same loss could be counted twice.
